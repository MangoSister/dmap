# Ray Tracing Animated Displaced Micro-Meshes

The missing piece of the DMM story: **what happens when you skin a displaced micro-mesh**. Written at AMD by the authors of [[Barczak et al. 2024 — DGF|DGF]] and [[Gruen et al. 2020 — Sub-Triangle Opacity Masks|Sub-Triangle Opacity Masks]] — i.e. the people arguing against DMM elsewhere, fixing it here.

## Problem

DMM displacements are **precomputed in a fixed rigging pose, along interpolated base-mesh normals**. Skin the base mesh with linear blend skinning and the reconstructed surface no longer reproduces the correct curvature: silhouette, normal and tangent-frame artefacts appear at exactly the places that bend — elbows, knees.

The failure is structural, not numerical. Displacements are static, so they cannot change amplitude or sign; when the surface bends, precomputed displacements sampled along the *interpolated skinned* normals produce sharp bends at the shared vertex and insufficient curvature per piece. A non-rigid motion such as a snake switching from curving outward to inward **cannot be expressed at all**.

The constraint the paper sets itself: fix this **without changing the DMM data structures or compression rate**.

## Core method

**Skin the matrices, not the geometry.** The baseline applies LBS to base positions and normals, then interpolates. This paper reverses the order:

1. Reconstruct the micro-triangles **in the rigging pose** they were created in.
2. **Implicitly assign bone matrices to micro-vertices** by barycentrically interpolating the *accumulated animation matrices*: `M_i(α,β,γ) = α·M_a0 + β·M_a1 + γ·M_a2`.
3. Apply that per-micro-vertex matrix to the reconstructed micro-vertex.

Because base-mesh simplification here uses **half-edge collapses**, the surviving vertex retains its original bone and weight assignment — sidestepping the re-rigging problem that [[Maggiordomo et al. 2023 — Micro-Mesh Construction|Maggiordomo's]] tweaked edge collapses would introduce.

**The Bézier insight.** Expanding the result shows both terms are **degree-two triangular Bézier patches**. An animated DMM is therefore exactly:

> one quadratic triangular Bézier **position patch**, plus a displacement-scaled quadratic triangular Bézier **normal patch**.

A pleasing side observation: applying the same construction with **zero displacement** already acts as a smoothing operator on the base mesh, much like Phong tessellation.

**Bounding.** Using the precomputed triangular min/max displacement hierarchy, define `P_min` and `P_max` patches. Every possible micro-vertex lies inside the **curved prism** between them, so by the convex-hull property of triangular Bézier patches the convex hull of the combined **12 control points** conservatively bounds the animated DMM. An OBB is fitted to those 12 points, adapting Munkberg et al.'s [2010] approach from rectangular to triangular patches. See [[Shell, prism and prismoid]].

**Tessellation-free traversal.** Following [[Thonat et al. 2021 — Tessellation-Free Displacement Mapping (TFDM)|TFDM]], traversal walks an **implicit quadtree** of bounding volumes generated by recursive patch subdivision — micro-triangles are never materialised. To avoid keeping a patch stack, sub-patch border curves are computed **directly from the top-level patches** by reparameterising each border curve and converting back to Bézier form. Sub-boxes are sorted front-to-back by box-centre distance.

An important technical divergence from TFDM, stated explicitly: **interpolated normals are not normalised here**, since normalisation would change only the magnitude of displacement, not its direction. TFDM does normalise, and therefore needs affine arithmetic to bound the result — a more complicated algorithm. See [[Min-max mipmap and conservative bounds]].

**Cheaper variant.** The interpolated-matrix scheme can be used purely to improve **tangent frames and normals** without changing the geometry — no better silhouettes, but it drops into an existing DMM pipeline unchanged.

## Results

DXR prototype on an **AMD Radeon 7900 XT**, full HD, primary rays only, three skinned SketchFab models (~150–400K triangles reduced to ~6K base triangles).

- **Quality:** the standard skin-then-interpolate DMM shows clear geometry and normal artefacts at the frog's elbow and knee; the interpolated-matrix approach "shows virtually no artefacts". It also removes the artefacts that half-edge-collapse base meshes introduce on their own.
- **Cost:** **~2.3–2.5× more expensive than ray tracing static DMMs** (e.g. frog at subdivision level 3: 2.75 ms vs 1.17 ms), attributed to algorithmic complexity and GPU shader register pressure.
- **Memory:** the frog's ~10 MB high-resolution model becomes ~0.5 MB as base mesh plus displacements, a 64× triangle reduction at subdivision level 3.

Displacement data is stored **once per object and reused by every instance even when animated**, so each instance costs only in proportion to base-mesh triangle count — and BLAS build/refit is proportional to the base mesh, with refit often sufficient.

## Limitations

- 2.3–2.5× the cost of static DMM tracing.
- **Watertightness between DMMs with non-uniform subdivision levels is not supported** and is listed as future work. See [[Watertightness and cracks]].
- Interpolating animation matrices is "in general not advisable when matrices contain rotations"; it is tolerable here only because animators hand-craft weights for a known motion range and the three vertices of a base triangle usually share similar matrices.
- The topological limitation remains: a DMM over one base triangle has **genus zero** and can only express displacements along interpolated normals — the same objection [[Barczak et al. 2024 — DGF|DGF]] raises.
- Proposed future work: a **hybrid** scheme using the cheap static algorithm in near-rigid regions, and tailoring simplification to leave more base triangles where deformation is strongest.

## Relation to other work

The most interesting position in the vault. Its authors are AMD's ray-tracing group; they cite [[Barczak et al. 2024 — DGF|DGF]] — their own work — as the alternative with "no topological limitations but lower compression density", noting DGF "is particularly geared at being consumed by future ray tracing hardware". So the same team published the DMM-improvement paper and the DMM-replacement paper in the same year.

It engages [[Thonat et al. 2021 — Tessellation-Free Displacement Mapping (TFDM)|TFDM]] most closely of all — borrowing its implicit-quadtree traversal while rejecting its displacement-texture sampling and affine-arithmetic bounding as incompatible with DMM compression ratios. It cites neither [[Thonat et al. 2023 — RMIP|RMIP]] nor [[Ogaki 2023 — Nonlinear Ray Tracing for Displacement and Shell Mapping|Ogaki]], despite both predating it.
