## 2026-09-08 — The object-level light sampler, recorded as the next direction for the lights

Discussion of T9's S7 result ([[Result — Emitter sampling in scenes]]) and of what the tessellation-free premise asks of the light sampler. Recorded in [[Plan — Path tracer with displaced surfaces]] (Next direction) and master plan §5A and Phase 3; not part of the current plans.

- **What T8 built and why.** One ks area light per emitting base triangle, because ks's `AreaLightShared` addresses lights by primitive and the BSDF-hit lookup finds a light by (instance, geometry, primitive); ks's `PowerLightSampler` then selects among all lights by a fixed table of their reported power, 2π × emission mass × √det G at the triangle's centre × area scale, with no dependence on the shading point. Every next-event sample in T8 and T9 goes through that table first and through our sampler, within the chosen triangle, second. The uniform line baseline is the exception: it casts a line through the emitters' box and uses the light list only to recognise emitters at the crossings.
- **What S7 measured.** With 4,416 emitting triangles the selection decides the variance: under MIS every kind is within 25% of the others and the product table, product descent and receiver-aware descent within 2%; the uniform baseline is only 5× worse at equal samples, against 40 to 70× on S6, because the informed kinds spend half their draws on triangles facing away or hidden behind the bunny, and the sun takes a fixed share. The receiver-aware descent cannot help: it redistributes samples inside the chosen triangle, and a ring triangle is 0.4 units across at 0.5 units or more from the bunny, so the receiver term hardly varies within it (the density check gives it the same percentiles as the product descent to three digits); on the rough-metal bunny its cos/r² target is also the wrong shape for a glossy lobe. Building the table costs a visit to every base triangle (the footprint fold along its edges, 200 ms and 172 MB on S7; the emissive texels themselves are enumerated once per tile when the emission tile is built).
- **The premise.** A tessellation-free emitter should not enumerate its emitting triangles ahead of time, and its sampler belongs to the object, or to the tile when the map repeats, not to the triangle. The per-triangle lights are a compromise with the renderer's interface, and the plan now says so at T8.
- **The design.** The direction. One ks light per (instance, displaced object) instead of one per emitting base triangle, with a single hierarchical descent over the object's texture domain. Above the tile level the nodes are cells of the object's uv box (for the station ring, 16 × 4 tiles), each carrying what a light BVH cluster carries: a spatial bound from the base triangles' boxes and the height range, a normal cone from the vertex normals and the height range, the emission mass, and a precomputed metric-weighted mass summed over the base triangles it covers, so that only the receiver term is evaluated per sample; descent weights are receiver-aware from the object's root. At and below the tile level the nodes are the shared pyramid's cells, stored once per tile however many repeats, with the metric composed per copy at the leaf as now. Cells that base-triangle edges cross are stored once in the object's tree with a list of pieces (triangle, clipped area, clipped mass), instead of once per triangle per level as the footprint tree does today. Overlapping or mirrored uv charts add one discrete choice of the copy with a deterministic weight, so a surface point's density is pdf_uv × P(copy | uv) / √det G of that copy. The pdf of a hit walks the same tree from the object's root, as `pdf_hit` walks the triangle's tree today, so MIS is unchanged. The pyramid, the emission tile, the wrapped indexing, admissibility from clipped mass, the probability floor, the leaf draw and the pointwise metric at the leaf all stay; the midpoint composition of √det G and its moment-channel remedy are independent of this change. The per-triangle lights stay as the baseline and for the pre-tessellated substitutes.
- **Can the descent support cells over several triangles?** Yes: the pdf contract needs deterministic node weights, a stored clipped area per leaf and the pointwise metric at the sample, none of which depends on the number of triangles under a cell; triangle edges enter only through the clipped pieces of straddling cells, which the footprint stores per triangle today and the object tree would store once. The one new cost is the metric estimate of a cell spanning many triangles, which must be precomputed per node (a sum of mass × √det G over its pieces) rather than evaluated per sample.
- **Claim and measurement.** What it claims: receiver-aware selection over the whole emitter without enumerating emitting triangles ahead of time, at a per-sample cost proportional to the tree's depth, with memory that depends on the base mesh only through the uv tree and not on a per-triangle footprint or table. The measurement that would show it is S7's ladder run again with the object-level sampler in the row set: today every kind sits within 25% of each other there because the selection above them is the same.
- Decision: recorded, not scheduled. T10 stays the next phase of the path tracer plan; whether the object-level sampler is built is a gate decision (master plan §7), and it belongs to Phase 3 if A is the headline.

## 2026-09-07 (night, T9) — T9 done: the emitter sampling ladder in scenes, and what a step map does to the product descent

[[Plan — Path tracer with displaced surfaces]] T9 is done: `test_emitter_ladder` runs the S8 ladder inside the path tracer's scenes, S6 (two panels, four emitting base triangles) and S7 (the station ring, 4,416 emitting base triangles and the sun), with the uniform line-casting baseline adapted to the traverser; tables and findings in [[Result — Emitter sampling in scenes]] (`code/data/results/t9_test_emitter_ladder.log`, `t9_ladder_s6.csv`, `t9_ladder_s7.csv`, `t9_flip_*.csv`, images under `t9_images/`).

- **The task.** Every sampler kind is set on the scene's displaced geometries and the area lights are rebuilt (`DisplacedGeometry::emitter_sampler`, changeable per run), so one scene build serves the whole ladder. Direct lighting at 200 × 150 by the integrator T8's MIS check used, now shared as `direct_lighting`, under next-event estimation alone and under MIS, at 64 samples per pixel against an MIS reference at 4096; relative MSE and FLIP over the whole frame, over the receivers (the pixels whose first hit is not an emitter) and over the far receivers (at least 0.25 units from every emitter's base mesh); the draw cost per light sample on the receivers; per kind the build time and the bytes the triangles own against the bytes shared through the asset (D5).
- **The gate.** Next-event estimation alone converges from below wherever an emitter lights a receiver at close range (T8), so it is judged at 2048 samples per pixel on the far receivers, MIS on the whole frame at 64, each mean within 2% of the reference plus two standard errors of its own noise, 2 √(relative MSE / pixels). Without the allowance the area-only descent on S7 failed at 2.6% with a relative MSE of 2.2 over 1,056 pixels, a 0.6-sigma deviation. The first far mask used the distance to the emitters' boxes and was empty on S7, since the bunny sits inside the ring's box; the mask now uses the distance to the base triangles, and an empty mask is an error. Both scenes pass.
- **The uniform baseline in a scene.** One line per sample through the union box of the displaced emitters, every crossing found by the scene's own intersection with the near distance advanced past each hit, so the walk reports the smooth surface point and normal and no mesh is involved; the estimator is the 2 × offset-area sum of `line_sampling.h`. Next-event only, as in S8. On S6 at 64 samples per pixel its relative MSE on the receivers is 10.3, 40 to 70× the informed samplers', and a line costs 1.8 µs; on S7 a line crosses the ring and the 144,000-triangle bunny and costs 8.4 µs, and its relative MSE at 2048 samples per pixel (1.57) is still worse than the informed samplers' at 64 under MIS.
- **The ordering on S6** (receivers, next-event only, 64 samples per pixel): product table 0.14, emission table 0.24, receiver-aware descent 0.31, product descent 1.15, area-only descent 6.1, uniform 10.3; under MIS 0.11, 0.17, 0.23, 0.86, 1.5. The A-MVP's ordering holds except that the product descent trails the product table 8× instead of 1.5×, and the receiver-aware descent, which shares its weights, does not lead. At 2048 samples per pixel the gap is 1.9×, so the loss sits in the tails, which FLIP discounts: on FLIP the product descent ties the emission table.
- **The cause is the map, not the chart or the footprint.** A density check, now part of the task, compares each kind's area density with the product table's at 20,000 random points of one triangle. On the sci-fi map the product descent's density is 0.10× to 5.6× the table's between the 1st and 99th percentile (worst 33×); with the rock map in the same scene at the same relief it is 0.62× to 3.3×, and there the product descent beats both tables on the receivers (0.105 against 0.128) with the receiver-aware descent best (0.085). The sci-fi map is plateaus and steps, and the descent's weight at each level is the emission mass times √det G from the node's Taylor plane at the cell's centre; on a step the plane's slope grows as the cells shrink, so the per-level ratios do not compose to the leaf's own √det G, which the table reads pointwise. This is the coarse-level midpoint composition the A-MVP measured as a 1.3 to 3.1× concession on rough content, made worse by steps; the moment channels recorded among the A-MVP plan's open decisions are the remedy, since they fold exactly. The emission table against the product table (0.2× to 2.2×) shows the metric factor itself varies 10× across this map's emitting texels, so the factor is worth having; it has to be estimated per cell better than the plane at the centre.
- **S7 says the per-triangle sampler is not what matters with many small triangles.** Under MIS at 64 samples per pixel every kind is within 1.1% of the reference and their relative MSE on the receivers, the bunny, spans 0.37 to 0.46; the product table, product descent and receiver-aware descent are within 2% of each other. The ring's base triangles are 0.4 units across and 0.5 units or more from the bunny, so the receiver term is nearly constant within a triangle, and the variance is decided by ks's selection among 4,416 lights by power alone. The receiver-aware descent works inside a triangle; a receiver-aware selection among triangles (a light BVH, master plan baseline 3) is the missing layer, orthogonal to it.
- **Cost per footprint under sharing** (D5). On S6 a panel triangle's product table is 1.0 MB against a 64 KB footprint tree, but the shared pyramid, node grid and emission boxes are 34 MB for four triangles, 8.6 MB per triangle amortized against 1 MB. On S7 the tables are 32.5 KB per light (140 MB) and the footprints 40 KB per light (172 MB), with 28.7 MB shared: a small triangle's table box is small, while its footprint tree still holds every straddling cell along three edges at every level, 96 bytes each. The hierarchy's memory case therefore needs both many triangles per tile and a compact footprint node; a float record with a byte for the overlap class would be 4× smaller, recorded, not built. Build times: tables 15 to 17 ms on S6 and 540 to 610 ms on S7, footprints 0.4 to 0.9 ms and 200 to 215 ms. Draw cost: tables 0.5 µs, descents 2.0 to 2.5 µs on S6 (nine levels) and 1.0 to 1.2 µs on S7.
- Also this phase: `direct_lighting.{h,cpp}` shared by `validate_path_tracer` and the ladder; a pixel mask on `compare_images`; `memory_bytes` on the samplers, the pyramid and the emission tile; `flip_t9.py`. `validate_path_tracer` reproduces T8's numbers through the shared integrator.

## 2026-09-07 (late night, T8) — T8 done: displaced lights in ks's path tracer, and a ks MIS bug found by the check

[[Plan — Path tracer with displaced surfaces]] T8 is done: `displaced_area_light` makes every emitting displaced object a ks area light through `DisplacedGeometry::create_area_light`, `path_trace` renders any scene of the specification through ks's `SmallPT`, and `validate_path_tracer` passes its three checks (`code/data/results/t8_validate_path_tracer.log`, `t8_validate_path_tracer_regression.log`, images under `t8_images/`).

The sampler works in object space and the instance transform is applied afterwards, with the area density converted by |det A| |A^-T n| at the sample's normal, so instancing and non-rigid placements are covered. The point inside the leaf comes from the render sampler's 2D Sobol draw, the descent from its rng, so the Sobol dimensions of every other light are unchanged (D7). Emission is the material's colour field at the sample's uv; the descent steers by its luminance tile.
- **The scene for light sampling is S6**, from the sci-fi map set the user added (`code/data/simple/scifi`): a 2048-texel EXR height field (values 0.0003 to 0.0123, so `midlevel = 0` and `strength = 8` give 0.1 units of relief over a 2 × 2 panel; `load_height_texture` now reads EXR through ks), an albedo map, and an emission map whose strips and patches cover 5% of the panel. - **The MIS check found a bias in ks.** On S6 at 1024 samples per pixel, next-event estimation only, BSDF sampling only and MIS through the `AreaLight` interface agree within 0.4% of each other's mean luminance, but `SmallPT` with one bounce was 2.0% brighter than all three (`t8_validate_path_tracer_mis_before_nee_fix_spp1024.log`). The cause is in `next_event_estimate`: its MIS weight used the light's pdf alone, while the emission-hit branch of `SmallPT::trace` weights by the pdf times the light selection probability, so the two weights of the same path summed to more than one; with several lights the BSDF-hit side is over-counted, the more so the smaller the selection probability. Every mesh light with more than one triangle is several lights, so every ks scene with an area light was affected; S6 with four triangle lights shows it at 2%. Fixed by carrying the selection probability on the next-event side (`nee.cpp`, one line, D7 item 7). After the fix `SmallPT` reproduces the MIS integrator's numbers exactly (same sample stream, same estimator: mean 0.56767, relative MSE 0.2597 in both).
- **The golden was re-rendered.** The fix changes every existing image with an area light, so the T7 golden could not stay; it was re-rendered on one thread with the fixed binary (`small_pt_spp16.exr`, the old one kept as `small_pt_spp16_before_nee_fix.exr`), and both gates pass against it: `validate_small_pt_regression` (`t8_validate_small_pt_regression_after_nee_fix.log`) and `validate_path_tracer` check (1), the same keys through `path_trace` with no `displace` entries (0 of 30,000 pixels differ). Whole-frame render time 2.1 s against 0.5 s on 32 threads; the walk is only reached by rays entering the displaced objects' boxes, so the per-ray factor of T5 dilutes to 4× per frame here.
- **Next-event estimation alone is heavy-tailed on S6.** The panels illuminate themselves at millimetre distances (a raised border next to a strip), where the area-sampling integrand has the 1/r² singularity: at 64 samples per pixel NEE only has relative MSE 20 against 1.4 for MIS and 5.0 for BSDF sampling; at 1024, 1.06 against 0.26 and 0.43. - **The substitute check on S6 found a T4 defect and two ks defects.** Run for the first time at full resolution (each panel's texel-aligned substitute has 524k triangles), the substitute first rendered 4.8× too dark, then, after the ks fix below, 8.9% too bright in the directly visible emission alone. ## 2026-09-07 (late night, later) — T5 done: traversal cost measured

[[Plan — Path tracer with displaced surfaces]] T5 is done: `test_traversal_cost` over S1 to S3, tables and findings in [[Result — Traversal cost study]] (`code/data/results/t5_test_traversal_cost.log`). The level policy default moves from k = 3 to k = 1.

- **Both never tests more nodes than the box**: 0 violations per ray and level on 300,000 rays. The slab alone is worse than the box everywhere (up to 40% more node tests, 10 to 56% more time), as T3 predicted for coarse cells: it is unbounded sideways.
- **The slab's gain is at the leaves.** Reading it at level 0 cuts the leaf tests by 40% on S1 and 10 to 20% on S2 and S3 with no extra node test; one level up saves a further 1 to 5% of the node tests; deeper levels save 1 to 4% more tests but cost slab evaluations, and on S3 both with k = 3 is up to 6% slower than the box. Both with k = 1 is within 1% of the fastest policy on every scene: 5% faster than the box on S1, 2% on S2, within 1% on S3. D2's default is k = 1; the five scene files follow.
- **Verdict part (2) fails on S3.** T3's crossover transfers to the walk's own nodes on S1 (level 2) and S2 (level 4) but not on S3, where T3 had found a marginal level-0 win (median ratio 0.82, the slab shorter on under half the rays) and the walk finds the box shorter at every level (ratio 1.12 at level 0). T3's rays all pass through a surface point inside the cell; most nodes a walk tests are cells the ray misses, where the slab's sideways extent costs more. The config now expects the measured crossover (−1 on S3) so the task serves as a regression; the run against T3's value is kept as `t5_test_traversal_cost_vs_t3.log`.
- **The leaf is the cost.** The two-triangle leaf is 21 to 45% faster than the certified Newton leaf on the same walk; the certified leaf bounds 18 to 28 sub-squares per leaf test. Speed work goes to the leaf first.

## 2026-09-06 (night) — General charts adopted; the clipping rule; PDE layout deferred

Decisions after the chart study ([[Result — Chart dependence study]]), recorded in the master plan §2.3, §4, §5A, §6 and the Status block, and in the path tracer plan's D5 and T1.

- **The representation is a general affine chart per triangle.** Per-vertex texture coordinates into a shared tile; per-face barycentric grids with shared edge rows are the special case where the chart is the identity. Adopted for the ray and sampling queries, where the bounds and samplers are covariant under the chart, derived and now measured. The master plan's §4 parameterization row and atlas row, the §5A bake sentence, and the §6 limitation are rewritten in place; §2.3 now names the chart among the per-triangle constants a query composes with.
- **The PDE layout is deferred, not decided.** Supporting general charts contains the per-face layout, so nothing has to be chosen before that work starts. What stays open is what the Laplace–Beltrami note already calls the research risk: cross-seam coupling for the lattice heat method, and crack-free geometry for the projected walk. Per-face tiles avoid both by construction and remain available.
- **Where clipping must be exact, and where it need not be.** Polygon clipping of every cell against the triangle is not required for correctness. Admissibility of a child is a boolean and uses the traversal's square-versus-triangle overlap test, exact with no polygon; a cell classified fully inside uses its full area. A straddling cell's weight uses an area estimate, the fraction of a fixed set of probe points inside the triangle with a positive floor, since weights steer variance only and need merely be deterministic and positive wherever mass exists. Only the leaf density needs the exact area of leaf ∩ triangle, one fixed-size clip of a square against three half-planes per sample, which costs less than the pointwise metric evaluation the same sample pays. The alternative that needs no clip at all, sampling the whole leaf square and treating out-of-domain draws as zero-contribution samples as the table baseline does, is unbiased too but wastes draws on triangles a few texels wide, so the leaf keeps the exact clip. Per-(node, face) weight caching, already recorded, makes every area exact at build time at no per-sample cost.

## 2026-09-06 (evening) — Does the method survive a general chart? Investigation and measurement

Prompted by the path tracer plan's T1: everything in the A-MVP code assumes the identity chart, and the question was whether the *method* does too, across all four applications. Measurement in [[Result — Chart dependence study]] (new `exp10_chart_skew.py`).

- **What the record says.** The identity chart was a decision, not an omission: master plan §4 adopts per-face barycentric grids, Ptex-style with shared edge rows, as the representation (marked crucial, because it removes the seam problem for the PDE applications), puts arbitrary UV-atlas compatibility out of scope, and §6 names a resampling preprocess as the bridge for authored assets. The A-MVP followed that. What the path tracer request changed is the requirement: real assets, glTF from Blender, and a TFDM comparison all live in the UV atlas, which TFDM handles natively. The genuine gap until today was narrower than the code's shortcut: no note stated that bound tightness had only ever been measured under the identity chart, and the kill criterion could have been chart-dependent.
- **What a chart is, and what it touches.** The affine map from barycentric coordinates to texture coordinates, one constant matrix and offset per triangle. The pointwise formulas are covariant under it, and the pyramid is a property of the texture alone. Checked numerically on 20 triangles and six charts: building the triangle in texture parameterization (edges [e1 e2] J, normal derivatives [Mu Mv] J, exact because P and M are affine) reproduces √det G up to |det J| at 7e-15, and leaves the stretch eigenvalue, the normal direction, obliquity, and the normalized integrability defect invariant to rounding. So the pointwise metric, the area element, the anisotropy, and both diagnostics are chart-free in real arithmetic.
- **Per application.** A, sampling: transfers fully; the plan's T1 does it, and the only new pieces are the polygon clip of a cell by a general uv triangle and the tile sharing. C, LoD error: the distortion measure ε was designed reparameterization-invariant (both metrics transform congruently), and the certificates propagate the same way; under an atlas a coarse node spans several faces, so certificates and composed weights become per (node, face) where per-face tiles let them nest. Bookkeeping, not validity. D, diagnostics: invariant, algebraically and numerically. B1, the lattice heat method: this is the application §4 was decided for. Inside one chart of an atlas the texel lattice is continuous across triangle edges, which is simpler than the per-face coupling; across chart seams it is not, and the Laplace–Beltrami note already calls the tessellation-free seam problem the actual research risk. B2, projected walk on spheres: the walks are seam-indifferent, but the surface must be crack-free, which an atlas with seams is not. Both PDE applications therefore keep per-face tiles, and the general-chart code contains that layout as the identity chart, so nothing is lost by supporting both.
- **One structural difference under an atlas.** Nodes and faces become two independent partitions. Every per-node quantity that depends on base constants (weights cache, coarse-level operators, LoD certificates, normal cones) is per (node, face). Under per-face tiles they nest. Memory and bookkeeping, not correctness.
- **The measurement.** exp10 reparameterizes the exp05 triangles by six charts with |det J| = 1 (identity, anisotropic scale at condition 2 and 4, rotation, shear at condition 2.6, mirror) and reruns the Phase 1 tightness machinery. Conservativeness: 0 violations under every chart. √det G and λmax: within 4% of the identity chart everywhere, including condition 4; the determinant-cancellation worry did not materialize, because affine propagation keeps the linear correlations. The normal cone: 19% looser under shear only, 3.09 against 2.59 at 8-texel cells on rock, which misses the Phase 1 kill ratio by 3%; within 4% under the other charts. Mechanism, reasoned: the cone radius uses per-symbol vector norms of the affine normal, and under shear the two symbols' deviations correlate. Fix: build the enclosure in an orthonormalized parameter frame per triangle, which affine forms allow without loss. A numpy propagation item, recorded in the result note, not part of the path tracer plan.
- **Recommendation, not applied.** Master plan §4 (the parameterization row and the atlas row) and §6 (the atlas limitation) should say: atlas assets are supported natively by the ray and sampling queries, as in TFDM, with the kill criterion measured under general charts; the PDE applications use per-face tiles, as in Ptex, because seam coupling is unsolved. That is a change to the paper's representation statement and is the user's call. The Status block and the Phase 1 outcome line are amended factually with the exp10 numbers; nothing else in the master plan is touched.

## 2026-09-06 — Path tracer with displaced surfaces: investigation and plan

The next deliverable is a CPU path tracer in `code/cpp` that ray traces displaced surfaces through the eight-channel pyramid, treats emissive displaced surfaces as lights through the A-MVP samplers, and mixes displaced objects with ordinary meshes in scenes from toml, OBJ, and glTF. This session read the TFDM paper note, the GfxExp reference implementation (`code/third-party/GfxExp/tfdm`, CUDA and OptiX), the ks renderer, and the current dmap code, and wrote the plan: [[Plan — Path tracer with displaced surfaces]], nine phases T1–T9, each with its own validation task. The traversal cost measurement the master plan has called the gating experiment since 2026-08-30 is its T4.

- **Parity is by construction, at level 0.** GfxExp's min-max mipmap takes the min and max over texel corners and folds by min and max; so does our h_min and h_max. At target level 0 the two structures are the same numbers. Porting TFDM's traversal skeleton (roots over the triangle's uv box, the stackless `down`, `next`, `up` walk with direction-sorted children, the triangle-versus-square discard, the affine-arithmetic AABB in a uv-aligned tangent space) and running it in box mode therefore *is* TFDM, and its node-test counts are TFDM's counts. That is the parity claim, and it needs no measurement.
- **The slab cannot be tested as a box.** The obvious way to use the Taylor channels, an AABB extracted from the affine form of h with the plane's coefficients, has vertical extent |gu| s + |gv| s + r, which is exactly the recovered interval the channel study found 5–29× too wide at coarse levels and only 1.06× at leaves. Nothing is gained. The tilt has to survive into the test. Written in the tangent space, where a surface point is (u + h n_x, v + h n_y, h n_z) and h is the plane plus a residual of size r, eliminating u, v and the residual leaves one linear inequality in (x, y, z) with interval coefficients from the normal's range over the cell. Along the ray that is an affine function of t with interval coefficients, and the admissible t is one interval. With a constant normal it is exactly two planes tilted by (gu, gv) and separated by 2r, the slab of the concept note; with a varying normal it widens conservatively. Cost: a few operations, the same order as the box test. This is the only place the traversal can beat TFDM, and the study's crossover says it can only do so below 8-texel cells, so the plan reads the slab only there by default and lets T4 settle the level.
- **Two exact oracles exist.** With the identity chart and the S2 mesh at n_tess equal to texels per side, the two-triangle leaf of TFDM describes exactly the S2 mesh, so embree over that mesh is an exact oracle for the whole traversal in that leaf mode. And every bound mode (box, slab, both, any level policy) must return identical hits, since all are conservative; a difference is a bug.
- **ks reuse, and its one gap.** `ks::Geometry` is abstract (`create_rtc_geom`, `compute_intersection`), so a displaced surface as an embree user geometry, one primitive per base triangle, needs no ks change and gets instancing and mixed scenes for free (embree passes the ray in instance space). Scene, materials, BSDFs, camera, render target, samplers, and the existing lights all reuse as they are. The gap is the light interface: `Light::sample` takes a point and one 2D random number, no shading normal and no sampler, and the MIS lookup returns the concrete mesh-triangle type. The receiver-aware sampler needs the normal, and the descent needs several random numbers. Decision: re-host `SmallPT::trace` (one hundred lines) as a dmap task with a dmap light sampler and next-event estimation, rather than edit ks, since dmap code stays out of the submodule. Recorded as an open decision: three small interface changes in ks would let `small_pt` run displaced scenes unchanged, and ks is the user's own code.
- **The A-MVP core assumes the identity chart everywhere.** `BaseTriangle::P(u, v)` is barycentric, the domain is u + v ≤ 1, `clipped_cell_area` is the closed form for that triangle, and each `DescentSampler` owns its pyramid and a per-triangle emission-sum pyramid. Real assets have arbitrary per-vertex texture coordinates and share one tile across thousands of triangles. Generalizing is one constant 2×2 Jacobian per triangle for the gradient, the metric, and the area factor, plus square-versus-triangle polygon clipping. The memory hazard is real: per-triangle pyramids or emission sums over a 256² tile for a 10k-triangle emitter would be gigabytes. So tile statistics are shared and the triangle's clipping enters at query time: weight = mean emission × clipped area × √det G, admissible when the cell's max emission is positive and the clipped area is positive. Certified-zero pruning stays exact through the max pyramid, and partial cells cost variance only.
- **Displacement convention moves to world units.** h = strength × (t − midlevel), Blender's Displace-modifier convention, per object, so one pyramid serves the whole object. The amplitude × mean-edge convention stays in the experiment tasks. Texels are node values with a wrapped or duplicated closing node, so a 2^L-texel texture gives 2^L leaf cells.
- **Blender to renderer.** glTF has no displacement, and Blender needs pre-tessellation to display it, but neither blocks the workflow. Block out with base meshes, add Subdivision and Displace modifiers for preview only, put the displacement map, strength, and midlevel in custom properties, and export glTF with Custom Properties on and Apply Modifiers off. Blender writes custom properties as node `extras`; ks's loader ignores them but exposes `traverse_gltf_scene_graph`, so dmap reads them and attaches displacement to the prototype by node name. A toml overlay keyed by object name is the canonical mechanism, works for OBJ, and wins on conflict; the glTF extras fill the same record. One record, two sources.
- **What is excluded, and why.** Level of detail above level 0: the node bounds the finest interpolant, TFDM's adjusted min-max bounds coarser-sampled surfaces, and the concept note's warning against mixing the two semantics stands. Per-instance strength (analytic, later). UV seams (TFDM's open problem). float32. GPU.
- **Plan shape.** T1 general chart and shared tile data; T2 node bounds in space; T3 traversal and leaf intersection; T4 the traversal cost study; T5 embree integration; T6 scene assembly and config; T7 the path tracer; T8 the emitter ladder in scenes; T9 the Blender workflow. T4 is a measurement and blocks nothing downstream. Master plan §7 item 2 now points at the plan.

## 2026-08-30 — Ray tracing on the pyramid: min-max h channel adopted, moment weights recorded

Session started from a practical question — a more realistic light-sampling scene needs a bigger base mesh, displacement map and emission map, so rendering cost matters — and turned into a diagnosis of two node channels. Measurements in [[Result — Pyramid channel study]]; new experiments `exp08_minmax_recovery.py` and `exp09_weight_estimator.py`.

- **Renderer survey first.** There is no tessellation-free traversal anywhere in the C++ code, and none was ever claimed: every ray in the project intersects the pre-tessellated S2 mesh through embree, per the S2 ground rule. ks itself has no displacement support and plumbs only `RTC_GEOMETRY_TYPE_TRIANGLE`, `SPHERE_POINT` and `INSTANCE`, so a custom intersector would be new work, not a switch. The S8 renderer is standalone (two hardcoded geometries, Lambertian floor, direct lighting), and the whole-mesh builder `build_displaced_mesh` has never been called — every call site tessellates one base triangle. The scaling wall is concrete: cc_torus at `n_tess = 128` is 210M micro-triangles and 5.4 GiB of vertex and index buffers before the BVH, and `n_tess = 64` already drops below the resolution the S8 MIS check depends on. A complex scene needs either a much coarser visibility mesh with a looser tolerance, or traversal on the representation itself.
- **Would traversal on the pyramid be competitive today? No, at coarse levels.** Put the Phase 1 recovery number in world units: on disp_rock at amplitude 0.2 the root shell is about 2 mean edges thick against a true 0.18, so the bounding volume at the top is twice the size of the triangle it bounds and rejects almost nothing. Two honest caveats recorded: traversal itself has never been measured, only bound widths, and at fine levels the slab is the better bound, so the net effect is unknown.
- **exp08 — where the recovery gap comes from.** The representation is not the problem: on an exact plane the recovery is 1.000000 at every level, and the six numbers can encode a min-max node outright (slope 0, `r` = the true half-range). The gap splits into the recovery formula with a nonzero slope (8.32× of the 10.93× at rock's root) and fold accumulation (the remaining 1.63×). The slope at coarse cells is the gradient hull midrange, 1.289 at the root against a true mean gradient of 0.008 — noise, not trend — and the fold is right to store it that way because `ru`, `rv` must be the exact gradient range for the metric. Also confirmed: the gradient channels are already exact, direct enumeration reproduces them to 3.6e-15, so all the loss is in the height channel.
- **Direct (non-folding) construction, priced and rejected as sufficient.** Because `h` is bilinear per texel, `h` minus a linear function is too, so the residual's extremes over any cell sit at texel nodes and the tightest node for a given slope is computable exactly by enumeration — no sampling, no Lipschitz padding, no risk to conservativeness. It passes every check (reproduces the closed-form leaf to 1.8e-15, never exceeds the folded `r`, 0 violations in ~90k dense samples) and costs `N² log N` instead of `(4/3)N²`, with levels independent and parallel. But it removes only the fold half, leaving 6–8× at the root, so it does not reach parity on its own. Kept as a recorded option for the slab, not adopted now.
- **Decision 1: add `h_min`, `h_max` as dedicated channels. Eight in total. Implement next.** Two numbers, min/max fold, exact at every level with no compounding, `(4/3)N²` to build. This gives TFDM/RMIP's bound *identically* rather than approximately, so coarse-level traversal is at parity by construction rather than by measurement, and the slab remains available as a second, better bound at fine levels. Master plan §2.2 and the concept note updated in place.
- **Why eight channels is not the ablation §1 says fails.** Raised as an objection and worth recording. A1 — the failing ablation — is *three* min-max channels consumed as intervals, and it has no slab. The eight-channel node is A3 (the same channels consumed as centred affine forms, measured ≡ A0 within 1%) *plus* the slab *plus* min-max h. It adds information and removes none. The note already localized the failure to interval evaluation rather than channel storage; midpoint-radius is in fact exactly the form centred affine propagation wants. Separately, §1's width-scaling argument does not bite either: as a scalar interval the node's recovery is always worse than min-max, and as a region in space the slab is thinner (0.0044 against 0.0324 at rock's texel cells). Different query shapes, neither subsuming the other.
- **exp09 — the S7 weight gap has a mechanism.** The descent weight's midpoint model underestimates a cell's true mean `√det G` in *every* cell from 16-texel cells up, by up to 2× on cobble at amplitude 0.2. Cause: the weight builds `∇h∇hᵀ` from a single gradient, discarding the gradient covariance over the cell, which is positive semidefinite and is exactly the cell's roughness; dropping it lowers `G` in the Loewner order and `det` is monotone there. So rough subtrees are under-weighted at every coarse level and the descent compounds it — the S7 diagnosis, now with a cause. The bias tracks the measured S7 gap monotonically across the three configurations (0.968 → tie, 0.719 → 1.39×, 0.526 → 3.05×).
- **Negative result: a better slope does not help.** Replacing the gradient hull midpoint with the cell's true mean gradient leaves rock unchanged and makes cobble worse. The problem is the nonlinearity, not the slope. This rules out the cheapest candidate fix and is why the answer has to be moments.
- **Decision 2: moment channels recorded, not implemented.** Every term of the metric formula is at most quadratic in `(h, ∇h)`, so `E[G]` is exact from seven base-independent moments that fold by averaging with no compounding. Measured, they cut the estimator error from 2× low to at most 1.43× high, with the overshoot guaranteed in sign by concavity of `√det` on the positive definite cone. They beat the midpoint from 8-texel cells up and overshoot below that on rough content, so the better estimator is level-dependent. Not implemented: estimator error is not variance, and whether this closes the S7 gap needs the S7 harness. Added to the A-MVP plan's open decisions alongside the fused product bake, which it competes with while keeping base independence.
- **A principle worth stating once:** these channels differ by fold law, and that explains every loss measured today. Min-max and moments fold exactly and can never accumulate; only the plane-plus-remainder slab compounds, and that is precisely the family whose value is a fine-level phenomenon where compounding has barely started. It also follows that channels can be stored per level rather than everywhere — moments buy nothing below 4-texel cells, the slab is useless at the root, and level 0 alone holds three quarters of the nodes.
- **Still unmeasured, and now the gating experiment:** traversal cost itself. Node tests and time against the S2 mesh as ground truth, for min-max only, slab only, and both. Recorded in the master plan §7 as part of the ray-tracing item.

## 2026-08-28 — S6 done: descent samplers

- `code/cpp/src/descent_sampler.{h,cpp}`: `DescentSampler` (hierarchical warping over the Taylor pyramid, one categorical draw per level, uniform draw in the leaf cell ∩ triangle by rejection, pdf per §5A: branch-probability product over clipped leaf area, divided by pointwise √det G) with the three weight variants — area-only, product, and product × receiver term — plus `TexelTableSampler` for the two non-hierarchical baselines (emission-only, §5A baseline 2; product table, baseline 5) on the S5 `DistribTable2D` machinery. `pointwise_fields` gained an overload taking (h, hu, hv) directly, so weights evaluate a node's Taylor plane through the exact metric formulas.
- Two design decisions beyond the plan text, both recorded here because they differ from the §5A pseudocode:
    - **Emission sum pyramid instead of the mip mean.** Per-cell weights use the integral of the piecewise-constant emission over cell ∩ triangle (leaf: E per texel × clipped texel area; fold: summation). This is additive, so on a flat patch the branch products telescope exactly to the normalized per-texel emission — the mip mean times clipped area is not additive and would not. It also gives certified-zero pruning for free at texel granularity: a zero sum proves E ≡ 0 on the cell, so probability zero is exact, not heuristic.
    - **Uniform-blend floor instead of the absolute ε floor.** Each admissible child gets (1−β)·w/Σw + β/n_admissible. The pseudocode's floor max(w, ε·Ē·|Ω|) is not scale-invariant: the receiver term's 1/r² would drown it far-field and turn the receiver-aware sampler into emission-only. The blend is scale-invariant and bounds every admissible probability below by β/4. β = 0 recovers exact proportionality (used in the flat-patch check).
- One implementation detail that matters for MIS: leaf offsets are drawn as float, so u = (i + ξ)/n_leaf re-quantizes to leaf i exactly (i + ξ is exact in double; dividing by a power of two is exact). The query-side pdf therefore walks the identical path and the re-walk is bit-exact, not approximately equal.

## 2026-08-28 — Hierarchy vs the product table; receiver-aware descent is feasible

Discussion before S5, folded into master plan §5A and the A-MVP plan (S6/S7). Three results.

- **How pbrt's bilinear-patch sampling transfers to displaced surfaces.** The algorithm is: draw (u,v) from an image-built 2D table, map through the parameterization, divide the uv pdf by the pointwise Jacobian. Only two ingredients are geometry-specific, and both are already in the C++ core: the map (S(u,v)) and the Jacobian (√det G). Sampling uv ∝ E gives the emission-only baseline (undersamples where displacement stretches area); building the table from per-texel E·√det G gives a full-resolution **product table** with the ideal area-domain target — the best any non-hierarchical sampler can place, added to §5A as baseline 5. pbrt's non-rectangular path (placement by bilinearly interpolated corner Jacobians, exact Jacobian in the pdf) is the approximate-placement-exact-pdf contract our descent scales up hierarchically.
- **Corrected cost claim (a concession).** A first framing had the table costing O(triangles × texels); the user pointed out that in a normal parameterization each triangle occupies its footprint, footprints partition the atlas, and per-footprint tables sum to O(atlas) — the same order as the pyramid. The inflated figure was an artifact of the experimental whole-tile-per-triangle convention and must not become a paper claim. What survives: the table bakes E·√det G, entangling content with base geometry, so it multiplies under **tile reuse** (per (face, tile) pair vs the pyramid's per tile) and goes stale under **dynamics** (deformation, amplitude edits: O(atlas) rebuild per change vs no rebuild — the edit-loop demo's axis). On static unique-atlas content the costs tie, and the far-field variance should tie too; the hierarchy's measured case there is receiver-aware descent and certificates. §5A now states this expected shape before the measurement, and S7's cost table is designed per footprint with a reuse sweep.
- **Receiver-aware descent: possible, with standard machinery.** The pdf is the product of the branch probabilities actually used, so weights may depend on the shading point; the two conditions are determinism in (node, receiver) — the MIS pdf query re-walks the same products — and positive probability wherever the contribution can be positive. Every factor of the true per-cell target ∫E·cosθ_s·|n_y·ω|/r² dA has an on-the-fly per-node estimate: emission mip mean, midpoint √det G, distance and receiver cosine from the cell's spatial bound (sub-triangle ⊕ certified height interval along the normal range — the light-tree cluster bound, derived from six numbers instead of baked), emitter cosine from the normal cone. Caveats found: certified cones saturate at π above ~8–16-texel cells on rough content, so the cosine factor only discriminates at fine levels; this is not exact solid-angle sampling (geometry factors are flattened only to their residual within a leaf — pbrt's spherical-rectangle inversion does not exist for a displaced map); weights cost ~5–10× a table lookup per sample, so equal-time numbers decide. Precedent to cite: Conty Estevez & Kulla's light tree, and pbrt's own corner-cosine warp. **Certified-zero pruning is the move estimates cannot make:** skipping a subtree with probability zero is unbiased only with a proof of zero contribution (certified spatial bound below the receiver's tangent plane; certified cone fully backfacing on a one-sided emitter). This makes the certified propagation port a dependency of the pruning variant and gives conservativeness a role inside the sampler itself.
- Plan changes: S6 grows the receiver-aware weight variant and the two table baselines plus a pdf-rewalk check; S7's ladder, expected-outcome statement, and cost-table design updated; the open decision on certified variants now names pruning.

## 2026-08-27 — S4 done: Ling et al. line-sampling baseline ported

- `code/cpp/src/line_sampling.{h,cpp}`: the uniform-line sampler and the surface-integral estimator, with embree all-hits casting over the pre-tessellated mesh replacing the paper's modified sphere tracing. A line is a uniform sphere direction plus a uniform offset on the perpendicular square of half-side ρ (the box half-diagonal) through the box centre; the estimator is ∫f dA ≈ (2·offset_area/M)·Σ_lines Σ_hits f, with M counting all sampled lines including box misses. The constant was rederived rather than copied: the official repo resamples until a fixed count of box-hitting rays, which folds the acceptance rate into its normalization; counting misses keeps the constant exact. All-hits uses a context filter that records and rejects every hit, then deduplicates by primitive (a triangle stored in several BVH leaves can be reported twice when every hit is rejected).
- **`validate_line_sampling` verdict: PASS**, four checks: (1) tilted rectangle of known area — estimate within 1.3 standard errors, which pins the offset-domain measure and the direction-sphere factor; (2) displaced submesh area vs the exact triangle sum — 0.3 SE at 0.1% precision; (3) a weighted integral with per-vertex weights (linear per micro-triangle, so the reference is exact) — 1.5 SE; (4) uniformity — total-variation distance 0.0017 between per-base-triangle hit shares and area shares, 775k hits. 12M lines total run in about a second.
- Two lessons from the first (failing) run: a `validate` task must exit with a code, not `ASSERT` — the ks assert handler blocks on the null stdin and hangs the run (all four validators now exit(1) on FAIL); and the test submesh must be a **contiguous** triangle patch — evenly-scattered picks spread 8 triangles over the whole asset, the sampling box covers everything, and the estimator starves (77 hits from 400k lines, though still statistically consistent, which is the estimator quietly working as designed).

## 2026-08-26 — Kill criterion revised after a per-application review; Phase 1 passes

- Question raised: does tightness at coarse cells (32–64 texels per side) matter for correctness anywhere, or only for efficiency? Reviewed each application:
  - **A, product sampling: variance only.** Every sample descends to a leaf, and the density is exact because √det G at the drawn sample is evaluated pointwise, never from a node. Coarse-level weights only split probability between subtrees; a loose weight misallocates samples, which is variance, never bias. The default weights use the node's midpoint model, not the certified interval, so interval width barely enters. The one correctness rule — positive weight wherever emission is positive — is the ε floor, independent of tightness.
  - **B1, texture-space operator: refinement depth.** A cell whose certificate does not close gets refined, so loose coarse bounds cost degrees of freedom, never wrong answers. Measured widths suggest certificates with ~10% tolerance close at 2–4-texel cells. The exposure is to the claim, not the correctness: if certificates only close near the leaf, "certified a-priori adaptivity" degenerates toward uniform fine resolution. Measure in Phase 2.
  - **B2, walk on spheres: efficiency, most sensitive.** Conservativeness keeps the estimator unbiased at any looseness, but the feature-size lower bound controls walk length (the PWoS paper's own figure shows 31 → 1819 steps as the bound shrinks). Our normal cones saturate at π above ~8–16-texel cells on rough content, so the cone localization only helps at fine cells. Worst case an order of magnitude in walk length, still never a wrong answer.
  - **C, LoD error: the one direct consumer of coarse cells.** The distortion certificate for mip level k is read per level-k cell. But LoD decisions usually compare levels 1–3 apart, and per coarse texel the relevant fine-side node covers 2–8 fine texels, where tightness is 1.5–2.2. Only certifying drops deeper than about 3 levels reads the loose 32–64-texel bounds.
  - **D, diagnostics: unaffected** (pointwise, no pyramid).
- Conclusion: correctness depends on conservativeness alone, which holds at every level; tightness at coarse cells buys only efficiency, and the applications read bounds for their *answers* at cells of roughly 1–8 texels. The old blanket criterion ("within 3× at the levels applications traverse") gated on cells no application depends on.
- **Criterion revised in the master plan §7, in place:** conservativeness is absolute (any violation kills); tightness must be within 3× of the true range at cells up to 8 texels per side on typical content; coarser cells are reported and priced per consumer in Phase 2. Flagged as sensitive there: the normal-cone localization of B2's feature-size bound, and LoD drops deeper than about 3 levels.
- **Under the revised criterion Phase 1 passes:** 0 violations, worst median ratio 2.77 (the cone on spot + disp_rock at 8-texel cells). Experiment 5's verdict logic updated to match and rerun; [[Result — Phase 1 Taylor pyramid]] updated. Phase 2 begins: the three MVPs, D first, and the C++ core.

## 2026-08-26 — Phase 1: Taylor pyramid built, kill-test run

- Implemented the pyramid in the numpy reference, per the session decision to keep Phase 1 in Python and start the C++ core with Phase 2 (master plan §4 updated in place). - **Kill-test outcome is mixed, left open for the gate.** On typical content (0.05×edge), median √det G tightness is 1.2× at texel cells, 1.6–1.9× at 4-texel, 2.7–4.1× at 32–64-texel cells. The criterion (more than 3× at traversed levels → rethink) is met at fine traversed levels and missed at the coarsest ones. Reasons recorded: the residual gap at coarse cells is inherent to the node — its six numbers bound h and the gradient by independent ranges, which also cover value combinations that never occur together on the surface — and not slack the propagation left (zeroing the base-form enclosure changes widths by under 5%; the ablations are never tighter). The Phase 2 consumers weigh in favor of proceeding: sampling weights (A) turn looseness into variance only; the certificate consumers (B1, C) refine until tight, so looseness costs refinement depth, not correctness.
- Three implementation findings changed the concept note, rewritten in place:
  - **Fold through interval hulls**: the parent gradient interval is the interval hull of the child intervals, with the slope at its midpoint; the parent offset and remainder are the midpoint and half-width of the child plane bounds evaluated at the quadrant corners. This replaced the child-mean plane and cut widths by about a third at mid levels.
  - **Propagation is intersected**: the affine route (squares anchored at the midpoint of the quadratic term's range) ∩ plain interval evaluation of the same node. Interval squares beat affine forms wherever a deviation straddles zero, which on rough content at coarse cells is everywhere; the affine route wins the correlated products. Eigenvalues need Weyl ∩ the affine discriminant ∩ the determinant division; the naive interval discriminant is useless.
  - **The §1 failure argument sharpened**: giving the same gradient two inconsistent worst-case values is a failure of interval *evaluation*, not channel *storage*. Min-max channels consumed as centred affine forms match the joint node's metric bounds (measured, within 1%); interval consumption costs 1.6–1.9× and twice the false alarms at fine levels. The joint node keeps the exact leaf and the O(s²) slab bound.
- Negative results, now in the notes: λmin has no meaningful per-node range certificate (rank-one structure pins it; width relative to value is order 1) — limits per-node anisotropy and conditioning certificates. Certified normal cones saturate at π above ~8–16-texel cells on textured content. Certificates of det G > 0 fail at coarse cells for every bound family tried. Min-max recovery is 5–29× looser than the exact channel near the root, so ray traversal keeps the height channel.
- Two implementation bugs caught by the conservativeness harness, worth remembering: sampling gradients exactly on cell edges attributes the neighbor cell's one-sided gradient to the wrong cell (truth grid now insets by 1e-9), and the cone cosine bound is invalid once the deviation radius reaches the centre length (must open to π).
- Cost as designed: 3.0× the two-channel min-max memory, one vectorized mipmap-style build pass (50 ms for a 512² tile in numpy), ~1.5 μs per node for a batched bound query.
